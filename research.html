Research Interests

My research investigates how adaptive AI systems can use physiological and behavioral signals to calibrate human-AI collaboration. I study how confidence calibration—the alignment between what people believe they know and what they actually know—can serve as a real-time adaptive signal, and how physiological sensing (EEG, HRV, EDA) can provide implicit measures of cognitive state to improve trust and decision quality in human-AI teams.

Current work spans three threads: (1) production-scale adaptive AI systems with confidence-calibrated algorithms; (2) cross-domain analysis of how adaptive algorithms generalize across professional domains; and (3) frameworks for integrating physiological biosignals with adaptive systems, bridging cognitive state estimation with brain-computer interface research.

The Safety–Agency Inversion: Longitudinal Multi-Method Evidence from Frontier Voice AI Companions

A. C. Perry

Targeting IJHCI (International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction); arXiv preprint May 2026

Documents a safety–agency inversion in frontier AI voice companions: models with stronger safety alignment consistently exhibit lower relational agency across 68 sessions and 83+ hours of naturalistic interaction. Five behavioral measures converge across three methodologically independent evidence streams. Introduces the HERVAC evaluation framework and releases the Her Dataset.

Current Projects

Cross-Domain Adaptive Engine Analysis

Final draft — targeting EDM 2026 Poster/Demo

Analysis of 16,450 production certification items across four professional domains revealing that item-level misconception concentration depends on a two-factor interaction between difficulty spread (σ) and domain base accuracy. An analytic predictor computed from item bank statistics alone ranks domains exactly—validated by simulation and robust across sensitivity sweeps. Enables domain-aware scheduling without learner data, motivated by the privacy-preserving on-device architecture.

Defense in Depth: Hardcoded Safety Architecture for AI Mental Health Systems

In progress — targeting AIES 2026

Evaluates a pre-AI hardcoded screening layer for crisis detection in AI systems processing mental health content. The architecture is immune to prompt injection, model drift, and API failures by design. Compares hardcoded pattern-matching against LLM-based classifiers using an adversarial test corpus, grounded in 357 lines of deployed production code from a sleep and dream wellness application.

Meridian Labs Platform

Ongoing — 12 production apps, 16,450+ MC items analyzed

Educational technology platform serving as both a commercial product and a research instrument. The adaptive engine’s six-algorithm cascade processes binary confidence judgments through asymmetric scoring, confidence-modulated spaced repetition, misconception detection, smart review, readiness prediction, and Bloom’s-aligned assessment. Cross-domain analysis of 16,450 items across all four deployment domains reveals how item bank properties interact with confidence-calibrated adaptation, with an analytic predictor ranking misconception concentration from item bank statistics alone.
