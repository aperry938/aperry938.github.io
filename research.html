Research

Research Interests

My research investigates how confidence calibration—the alignment between what learners believe they know and what they actually know—can serve as a primary adaptive signal in intelligent tutoring systems. I study how metacognitive awareness, combined with spaced repetition and misconception detection, produces measurably different learning outcomes across professional certification domains.

Current work spans three threads: (1) confidence-calibrated adaptive learning engines deployed at production scale; (2) cross-domain analysis of adaptive algorithm generalization; and (3) frameworks for integrating physiological biosignals as implicit confidence measures, bridging adaptive learning with brain-computer interface research.

Current Projects

Cross-Domain Adaptive Engine Analysis

Draft complete — targeting EDM 2026

Quantitative analysis of a confidence-calibrated adaptive engine across four professional certification domains using per-item difficulty and Bloom’s taxonomy data from 16,450 production items. Key findings: score-level metacognitive differentiation is domain-invariant (Cohen’s d > 2.0 across all domains, 4.5× misconception ratio), while item-level behavior diverges predictably—difficulty distribution shape (σ varying 2× across domains) predicts where misconceptions cluster, and higher Bloom’s tiers amplify overconfidence penalties through a measurable Dunning-Kruger gradient.

Biosignal-Informed Adaptive Learning

In progress — targeting AIES 2026

Framework paper proposing integration of physiological confidence signals (EEG, HRV, EDA) with confidence-calibrated adaptive engines. Addresses the fundamental limitation that learners who most need calibration support are least able to accurately self-report confidence. Includes ethical analysis of biosignal monitoring in educational contexts.

Meridian Labs Platform

Ongoing — 12 production apps, 16,450+ MC items analyzed

Educational technology platform serving as both a commercial product and a research instrument. The adaptive engine’s six-algorithm cascade processes binary confidence judgments through asymmetric scoring, confidence-modulated spaced repetition, misconception detection, smart review, readiness prediction, and Bloom’s-aligned assessment. Cross-domain analysis of all 12 apps confirms the engine generalizes without modification—validated via 20,000 per-item simulations showing Cohen’s d > 2.0 for metacognitive differentiation across all four deployment domains.
