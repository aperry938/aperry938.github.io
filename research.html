Research

Research Interests

My research interests are in human-AI interaction, AI safety, and adaptive learning systems. I’m interested in how AI, as it becomes part of daily life, is changing the way people behave, communicate, and interact with each other, and in the risks that come with systems built to please their users. In parallel I build adaptive learning software, where a learner’s confidence in each answer shapes what they see next.

Preprints and manuscripts

Pre-registered study

Judgment-Free but Not Risk-Free: How Perceived Emotional Safety with AI Companions Relates to Human Self-Disclosure and Help-Seeking

A. C. Perry

Pre-registered on OSF (osf.io/5xzjs). I compared 30 people who talk to AI about personal matters with 25 non-users on the Self-Disclosure Index and the PRCA-12. AI users were far more willing to open up to a new acquaintance (d = 1.08), and 57% said they had sought professional help for something first raised with AI. Whether feeling safe with an AI carries into disclosure to people is an open question.

Preprint · under review

The Epistemic Harm of AI Sycophancy: When Agreement Undermines Justified Belief

A. C. Perry

Preprint on Zenodo; a revised version is under review at AI and Ethics

Argues that sycophantic AI damages users’ beliefs through three mechanisms (confidence inflation, challenge atrophy and empathic substitution), drawing on social epistemology and on recent experiments by other researchers.
