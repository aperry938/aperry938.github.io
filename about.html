Early Life & Military Service

Born at Fort Riley, Kansas and raised in a Bavarian village of 2,500 as the only mixed-race child. Navigating four distinct cultural environments before age eighteen—rural Bavaria, multicultural Kaiserslautern, an American military high school, and U.S. Air Force Basic Training—cultivated an observational capacity for human behavior that would later become a research orientation. Two years of ESL courses despite American parentage meant learning English analytically from the outside—an early sensitivity to the mechanics of language that now informs computational work in NLP and authorial analysis.

As a C-17 Aircraft Loadmaster, Anthony flew 455 combat sorties and logged 1,977 flight hours across Operations Enduring Freedom, Iraqi Freedom, and New Dawn—managing the airlift of 13.5 million pounds of cargo and 9,400 passengers. He directed special forces combat insertions, aeromedical evacuations, dignified transfers of fallen service members, and presidential support missions transporting the Secret Service and motorcade for Barack Obama. His service earned the Air Medal and the Humanitarian Service Medal for Hurricane Sandy relief operations reaching 8.2 million people.

The Catalyst

The transition from military to civilian life was not seamless. After four years of sustained combat operations, Anthony was medically separated with a significant disability rating, the majority attributed to PTSD. Conventional treatment proved insufficient, prompting a search for alternative approaches to nervous system regulation that would ultimately redirect his entire intellectual trajectory.

That search led first to the body—using principles of progressive overload and neuroplasticity to actively retrain a dysregulated autonomic nervous system. It led next to consciousness itself: in 2017, he traveled to the Peruvian Amazon to work with Shipibo healers in traditional Ayahuasca ceremony, seeking what conventional medicine had not provided. The experience fundamentally altered his understanding of the relationship between mind, body, and subjective experience—shifting from a purely materialist framework toward a view of consciousness as something that could be observed, mapped, and potentially modulated.

This period—from system failure to somatic repair to phenomenological insight—is the origin of every research question he now pursues: How does cognition break down under extreme stress? How does the body participate in its own healing? And can intelligent systems be designed to support these processes at scale?

Research Foundation

The physical dimension has always been central—from European track & field qualifications to the conviction that the human body is not fixed but plastic, trainable, and measurable. At San Diego State University, graduating with honors and Distinction in Kinesiology, and California Baptist University, Anthony studied how the body and brain acquire skill under conditions of stress and uncertainty.

His master’s thesis designed a two-arm randomized controlled trial investigating yoga intervention for overuse injury prevention in distance runners, involving multimodal data collection (EMG, goniometry, Oslo OSTRC questionnaire), full IRB protocol development, and a systematic review of 30+ peer-reviewed articles spanning biomechanics, neurophysiology, and motor control. The insight: static, analog interventions could not model the dynamic, non-linear fluctuations of human physiology in real time—a limitation that catalyzed the pursuit of computational approaches.

Embodied Practice & Phenomenological Inquiry

A 500-hour Registered Yoga Teacher certified across Ashtanga, Vinyasa, Yin, Yoga Nidra, and Pranayama. Completed formal training in Bali and remained for three years of continued practice and deepening study—followed by periods of solitary work in the mountains of Costa Rica and the Andes, completing graduate research remotely while immersed in contemplative practice.

This extended immersion represents more than credentialing. It is a sustained first-person investigation of embodied experience, interoceptive awareness, and the boundaries of consciousness—a phenomenological method that complements and grounds computational inquiry. The lived practice of attention, proprioception, and breath regulation directly informs the design of AI systems that interact with human bodies and minds.

Engineering & Current Work

Completing an M.S. in Computer Science (AI/ML) at Western Governors University, expected June 2026. Founded Meridian Labs: 10 production apps across 4 brands, 25,000+ questions, and 8 proprietary algorithms grounded in 30+ peer-reviewed citations in cognitive science and testing psychology. The AI-augmented single-researcher workflow—where one person orchestrates LLMs for architecture design, content generation, and quality control—is itself a subject of inquiry into the future of human-AI collaboration.
