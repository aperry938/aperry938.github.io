Early Life & Military Service

Anthony grew up in Freihung, a small Bavarian village of 2,500, raised by his German mother and grandparents. As one of three mixed-race siblings in a traditional, monocultural community, he learned early to read the unspoken codes of the culture around him—observing what was implicit for others because, for him, it had to be made explicit. That pattern of navigating between worlds continued through rural Bavaria, multicultural Kaiserslautern, an American military high school, and eventually U.S. Air Force Basic Training. Two years of ESL courses—despite American citizenship—meant learning English as a system to be decoded, an experience that shaped a lasting analytical relationship with language.

At twenty, he enlisted as a C-17 Aircraft Loadmaster—responsible for the cargo integrity of a 585,000-pound aircraft. Over four years he flew 455 combat sorties and logged 1,977 flight hours across Operations Enduring Freedom, Iraqi Freedom, and New Dawn. He directed special forces combat insertions, aeromedical evacuations, dignified transfers of fallen service members, and presidential support missions for Barack Obama. His service earned the Air Medal and the Humanitarian Service Medal for Hurricane Sandy relief.

The Catalyst

The transition out of the military was not seamless. After four years of sustained combat operations, Anthony was medically separated with a significant disability rating, the majority attributed to PTSD. Conventional treatment proved insufficient, and what followed was a search for alternative approaches to nervous system regulation that would quietly redirect his entire intellectual trajectory.

That search led first to the body—applying principles of progressive overload and neuroplasticity to actively retrain a dysregulated autonomic nervous system. It led next to consciousness itself: in 2017, he traveled to the Peruvian Amazon to work with Shipibo healers in traditional Ayahuasca ceremony. The experience fundamentally altered his understanding of the relationship between mind, body, and subjective experience—shifting from a purely materialist framework toward a view of consciousness as something that could be observed, mapped, and potentially modulated.

This period—from system failure to somatic repair to phenomenological insight—is the origin of every research question he now pursues: How does cognition break down under extreme stress? How does the body participate in its own healing? And can intelligent systems be designed to support these processes at scale?

Research Foundation

The physical dimension has always been central. A competitive athlete who qualified for European track & field events, Anthony carried the conviction that the human body is not fixed but plastic—trainable and measurable—into his academic work. He studied kinesiology at San Diego State University, graduating with honors, and earned his Master’s at California Baptist University, where he investigated how the body and brain acquire skill under conditions of stress and uncertainty.

His thesis designed a two-arm randomized controlled trial examining yoga intervention for overuse injury prevention in distance runners—multimodal data collection (EMG, goniometry), full IRB protocol, and a systematic review of 30+ peer-reviewed articles in biomechanics, neurophysiology, and motor control. The central insight: static, analog interventions could not model the dynamic fluctuations of human physiology in real time. That limitation pointed toward computation.

Embodied Practice & Phenomenological Inquiry

Anthony completed a 500-hour yoga teacher certification in Bali—training across Ashtanga, Vinyasa, Yin, Yoga Nidra, and Pranayama—and remained for three years of continued practice and deepening study. That period extended into solitary work in the mountains of Costa Rica and the Andes, completing graduate research remotely while immersed in contemplative practice.

This was not a retreat from intellectual life but a different kind of research—a sustained first-person investigation of embodied experience, interoceptive awareness, and the boundaries of consciousness. The lived practice of attention, proprioception, and breath regulation directly informs the design of AI systems that interact with human bodies and minds.

Engineering & Current Work

Completing an M.S. in Computer Science (AI/ML) at Western Governors University, expected June 2026. Founded Meridian Labs—a platform of 10 production apps and 25,000+ exam questions, built on proprietary algorithms grounded in cognitive science and testing psychology. The AI-augmented single-researcher workflow, where one person orchestrates LLMs across the full product lifecycle, is itself a subject of inquiry into the future of human-AI collaboration.
